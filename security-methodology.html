Security methodology

Evidence before conclusions.

SiteShield is designed around explicit authorization, bounded observation, preserved evidence lineage, scheduled monitoring, and deterministic verification.

Authorization is a gate, not a suggestion.

Passive public checks are kept non-intrusive. Active testing requires verified ownership, active scan authorization, exact scope verification, and an allowed scan policy. Missing or ambiguous authorization fails closed.

Agency access does not automatically authorize arbitrary client infrastructure. Authorization provenance remains attached to each asset.

Evidence model

Observed facts stay separate from interpretation.

Scanner and deterministic observations establish technical evidence. AI may correlate, summarize, prioritize, and explain, but it does not create vulnerability facts or erase disagreement between sources.

Monitoring cadence

A paid plan has a defined minimum rhythm.

Business performs scheduled passive monitoring of the activated public website at least once every 7 days. Pro performs scheduled passive monitoring at least once every 24 hours and may include additional authorized website, API, or ecommerce surfaces agreed during onboarding.

Reported fixes or material changes can trigger an earlier manual or event-driven recheck. Active testing never inherits authority from the clock: every active check still requires the exact site's ownership, scope, and authorization gates to be satisfied.

If a safe check cannot be performed because the site, scope, or authorization state has changed, the affected check is paused rather than silently expanding scope.

Finding lifecycle

Open is not resolved until verification says so.

Findings move through OPEN, ACKNOWLEDGED, FIX_IN_PROGRESS, and VERIFYING before they can become RESOLVED. ACCEPTED_RISK and FALSE_POSITIVE are explicit terminal states with different meanings.

SiteShield does not claim certified penetration testing, PCI ASV status, compliance certification, guaranteed security, or complete vulnerability coverage unless those claims receive separate qualification.

Self-monitoring

SiteShield is included in its own monitoring program.

The SiteShield public surface is monitored within an explicitly authorized scope so deployment changes can be observed and rechecked. Self-assessment is not independent validation.
